AI Receptionist for Real Estate Agents in Kenya: Property Enquiries and Viewings

Property agents can receive calls while showing homes, meeting clients or travelling between sites. An AI receptionist can help capture the caller’s requirements and answer approved general questions, while agents verify listing details and confirm viewings.

A practical guide for Kenyan business owners · Updated 14 November 2026 · Published by TWIGA

In brief

For a real estate business, an AI receptionist can collect the property a caller is asking about, their preferred location and the next step they want. It should not promise that a listing is still available, quote unverified terms or confirm a viewing unless the agency’s live information and staff process support it.

Why property enquiries need a clear first response

A caller may ask whether a property is available, request rent or sale details, ask about location, or want to arrange a viewing. If an agent is already with a client, a clear first response and a useful enquiry record can help the agency follow up without relying on memory.

What the receptionist can capture

  • The listing or property reference the caller is asking about.
  • The caller's preferred contact method and suitable time to respond.
  • The general area, property type and broad requirements.
  • Whether the enquiry is about renting, buying, selling or management.
  • Whether the caller is requesting more information or a viewing.

Ask only for details needed to route the enquiry. Avoid collecting identity documents or financial information in a routine first-call flow.

Keep property facts current

Listing status, rent, sale price, deposit terms, photographs and viewing arrangements can change. The agency should identify the approved source for each fact and set an update process. If a listing cannot be checked live, the receptionist should say an agent will verify the details before making a commitment.

A practical viewing request workflow

  1. Identify the property or search request.
  2. Capture the caller’s broad requirements and contact detail.
  3. Share only verified information from the current listing.
  4. Collect a preferred viewing window as a request, not a confirmed appointment.
  5. Send the enquiry to the responsible agent to check availability, access and any viewing rules.
  6. Confirm the arrangement through the agency’s normal process.

Protect clients, tenants and property owners

Do not disclose tenant names, private contact details, access codes, alarm information or security arrangements to callers. Staff should verify a caller’s authority before sharing sensitive property or transaction information. Escalate unusual requests, disputes and offers that require negotiation or legal review.

Questions for a real estate provider

  • Can the receptionist read from an approved listing feed or CRM?
  • How quickly can the agency mark a listing as unavailable?
  • Can the system record a viewing request without falsely confirming it?
  • Who receives new buyer, tenant and seller enquiries?
  • How are call recordings and contact details protected?
  • Can an agent take over when a caller asks for a person?

Test common enquiries before launch

Use test listings and test callers. Ask about an available property, a listing that has just been taken, a viewing outside business hours and a caller who wants to negotiate. Check that the receptionist distinguishes confirmed facts from details that need agent approval.

Use enquiry records to improve follow-up

Review whether agents receive the property reference, the caller’s requirements and the requested next step. Track enquiries that are waiting for confirmation and make sure they have an owner. Keep the process simple enough that staff actually use it.

Start with a narrow workflow

A useful first version might capture calls about listings and viewing requests, while agents remain responsible for live status, negotiation, qualification and final confirmation. Test the flow with real scenarios and expand only when the handover works consistently.

Common questions

Can an AI receptionist arrange property viewings?

It can collect a preferred date and time. A viewing should be confirmed only after the agent checks the listing, access and schedule, or after a tested calendar integration completes the booking.

Can it answer whether a property is still available?

Only if it checks an accurate, current listing source. When that connection is not available, it should record the question and ask an agent to confirm.

Does the receptionist replace a letting or sales agent?

No. It can support first responses and enquiry capture, but agents remain responsible for advice, negotiations, property access, verification and transaction decisions.
