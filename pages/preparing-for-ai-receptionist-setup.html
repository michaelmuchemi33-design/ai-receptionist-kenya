What to Prepare Before Setting Up an AI Receptionist

A checklist for getting your business details, call flow and phone provider requirements ready before activation.

Business information

Prepare your official business name, services, product list, current prices, service areas, opening hours and common customer questions.

Call handling instructions

Decide how calls should be greeted, what information can be shared, which enquiries need a human and what should happen after hours.

Telephone connection and testing

Confirm which phone number and call provider you plan to use. Features such as transfers, outbound calling and after hours handling depend on the provider and configuration, so test them before going live.

Prepare an approved knowledge base

Gather the facts customers ask for most often and check them with the people responsible for each area. Include service descriptions, products, prices, location, opening hours, delivery or collection options and important policies. Separate confirmed facts from information that changes frequently. Do not add passwords, payment details or private customer records to general call instructions.

Map the common call paths

Write down what should happen when someone asks about a price, wants an appointment, has a complaint, requests a quotation or calls outside business hours. Identify which questions can be answered immediately and which need a staff member. Decide who receives a handover and what details should be captured so the caller does not have to start again.

Confirm telephone and integration requirements

Discuss the existing business number, phone provider, call forwarding options and required integrations before activation. Ask whether inbound calls, transfers, voicemail, appointment tools and records are supported in the proposed setup. These functions depend on the provider, device permissions and configuration; do not assume every feature works with every SIM or telephone plan.

Test realistic scenarios before launch

Use a staff member to test the questions customers actually ask. Include an unclear request, an outdated price, a caller asking for a person and a call made after closing time. Check that the receptionist admits when it does not know something, collects the right details and follows the intended handover route. Make corrections before customers rely on it.

Review the first weeks of use

After launch, review sample conversations and enquiry records with the people who handle follow-ups. Look for repeated questions, failed handovers and information that needs updating. Keep the initial scope focused, then add more tasks after the existing call flow is working reliably.
