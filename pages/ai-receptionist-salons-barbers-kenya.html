AI Receptionist for Salons and Barbers in Kenya: Keep Bookings Moving

A barber is in the middle of a fade. A stylist is washing a client's hair. The person at the front desk is collecting payment. Then the phone rings. For many salons and barbershops, that is a familiar moment: the caller wants a quick answer, but everyone is already helping someone in front of them.

TWIGA Editorial Team·6 min read·22 November 2026

The phone often rings at the busiest time

Salons and barbershops sell time as much as they sell a service. Customers want to know which treatments are available, how long something takes, what it costs and whether there is a suitable appointment. They may call before work, during a lunch break or while travelling across town. If nobody can answer, they may try another salon whose next step is clearer.

The challenge is not that staff do not care. Their hands are often busy. A barber cannot stop midway through a cut every time the phone rings. A stylist may be with a client who deserves their full attention. A small team may not have a separate receptionist for every shift.

An AI receptionist can help cover that first response. When the configured phone provider routes a call to it, the receptionist can greet the caller, answer supported questions and collect details for the team. It gives the customer somewhere to start while staff continue serving the people who are already in the salon.

Give customers a useful first answer

Start with the questions your team hears every day. These might include whether the salon is open on Sunday, whether it offers braids or colour, how to find the shop, whether children's cuts are available, or what a particular service costs.

The receptionist can answer those questions when the owner has supplied accurate information. It should not invent a price, claim a stylist is free or promise a service that the salon does not offer. If the information is not available, it can explain that the team needs to confirm and collect the customer's details for follow-up.

That consistency matters for customers who are comparing several salons. A clear answer about location, opening hours and the next step can be more helpful than a vague promise that somebody will call back. It also reduces interruptions when the same basic questions arrive repeatedly.

Turn appointment questions into organised requests

Appointment enquiries often begin with a question rather than a confirmed booking. A customer might say, “Can I come at five?” The salon still needs to know the service, the required time, the preferred staff member if relevant and whether the slot is available.

An AI receptionist can collect the service requested, the customer's name and phone number, and the preferred date and time. If TWIGA is connected to the salon's booking calendar and that integration is enabled, it can check the available schedule and follow the configured booking process. Without that integration, it should record the request for staff confirmation rather than pretending the appointment is confirmed.

Before any booking is created, the important details should be repeated to the customer. That gives them a chance to correct the service or time. The salon can then see a clear record and know whether the booking is confirmed or still waiting for a human check.

Let staff handle the decisions that need experience

Not every salon conversation should be automated. A customer may have an allergy concern, want advice about a chemical treatment, complain about a previous service or ask for a style recommendation that depends on their hair and personal preferences. Those conversations need a trained professional.

The receptionist should recognise the difference between a routine question and a situation that needs a person. It can collect a short message, explain that the team will review it and avoid giving medical or technical advice beyond the salon's approved information. This protects the client and the reputation of the business.

It is also important to respect customer privacy. Collect only what is needed to respond to the enquiry. Do not ask for payment PINs, passwords or one-time codes. If a customer does not want to provide a detail, the system should still handle the conversation politely.

Keep the service list and prices accurate

Write down the salon's current services and prices in one place. Include the names customers use, not only internal shorthand. Add opening hours for ordinary days and special holidays, branch locations, accepted booking methods and any rules about deposits, cancellations or late arrivals.

Decide which requests are safe for the receptionist to handle. For example, it may answer whether a service exists and capture a preferred slot. A stylist should still confirm a complex colour consultation, a change to a treatment plan or a complaint. Keep this list simple enough that staff can maintain it.

When a service or price changes, update the approved information before the new details are advertised. An AI receptionist should not continue quoting a price that was correct last month. A named staff member should own these updates, just as they own the salon's price list and booking diary.

What a good salon workflow looks like

A practical call journey might look like this: the caller asks about a service, the receptionist explains the published options, collects a name and preferred time, then checks the calendar if the salon has connected one. If the time is available and the customer confirms the details, the configured booking action can save it. If anything is unclear, the receptionist creates a handover for staff.

For a barber, a similar process might cover a haircut, beard trim or children's cut. The owner can provide the duration and published price for each service, while the team keeps control over unusual requests and the final quality of the service. The goal is not to make every conversation sound the same. It is to make sure the routine details are not forgotten.

The salon should still decide who checks new requests, who handles cancellations and how staff see the day's appointments. An AI receptionist is part of the operating process, not a replacement for a well-managed diary.

Check whether the process is working

Review the number of calls answered, appointment requests recorded, confirmed bookings, handovers and missed follow-ups. Ask staff whether the notes include the service and preferred time they need. Ask customers whether the first response was clear and whether they understood what was confirmed.

If most calls are about prices, make the price list easier to maintain. If customers keep asking for a particular stylist, add an approved explanation of how staff availability is confirmed. If bookings are being requested outside opening hours, decide how the salon wants those requests handled the next morning.

Do not judge the system only by the number of calls it answers. A good process protects the experience inside the salon as well as the caller's experience on the phone. The best result is a team that can focus on the client in front of them while new enquiries are captured accurately.
